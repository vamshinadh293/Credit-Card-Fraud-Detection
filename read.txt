"Because fraud represented only about 0.17% of transactions, a majority-class classifier could achieve approximately 99.8% accuracy while detecting no fraud. This demonstrated why accuracy was inappropriate as the primary evaluation metric."
"Because fraud was only 0.17% of transactions, accuracy was misleading. I used class-weighted Logistic Regression and evaluated precision, recall, F1 and PR-AUC. I then varied the decision threshold from 0.20 to 0.60 and quantified the resulting false-positive and false-negative trade-offs. Finally, I introduced different costs for FP and FN to select the threshold based on operational impact."
"I evaluated thresholds from 0.20 to 0.60 using both classification metrics and operational cost. With an assumed false-positive cost of ₹100 and false-negative cost of ₹1,000, the 0.60 threshold produced the lowest total cost among the tested thresholds. However, the threshold is not universally optimal because the result depends on the actual business cost assigned to false positives and false negatives."
"For Random Forest, I evaluated thresholds from 0.20 to 0.60. Increasing the threshold reduced false positives and increased precision, but it also increased false negatives and reduced recall. When I introduced an assumed cost of ₹100 for a false positive and ₹1,000 for a false negative, threshold 0.20 had the lowest total cost among the tested thresholds."
"Using an assumed cost of ₹100 per false positive and ₹1,000 per false negative, threshold 0.40 was selected on the validation set. Thresholds 0.40–0.60 produced identical validation predictions and therefore identical cost and F1."
